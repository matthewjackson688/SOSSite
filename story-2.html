Deaf Link Uganda Community Work

Communicating Without Language

Deaf Link Uganda (DLU) has just returned from Eastern Uganda after several days' fieldwork in Kumi & Ngora Districts. Its drought out there, starvation: nothing grows, yet the people, farmers who depend on their land for subsistence, continue to hope for the rains. It's an incredible miracle that they are resilient and finding ways to survive, against the odds. The weather is terribly harsh, the heat relentless. But that doesn't drive away the human survival spirit: the smiles, humility and understanding. This for me is truly humbling. Here are admirable Deaf people who don't know Sign Language, have never been to school, yet so knowledgeable about the changing weather patterns. They are far more empowered in finding solutions to challenging circumstances. Although we struggle to communicate, our humanity connects us, irrespective of our status or level of education. We're One; defined by our Humanness, which is paramount.

Deaf Link Uganda and DEAR Day

A March highlight to share - I don't know how many of you are familiar with "DEAR Day." DEAR stands for: "Drop Everything And Read" and seeks to promote a reading culture among communities in different parts of the world. It originates in America and last year Deaf Link Uganda (DLU) participated for the first time; mobilising and working with Deaf children in Schools for the Deaf in various Reading Activities. It was a great success. DEAR Day, is an annual event. This year, it was held on 15th March. DLU worked with Deaf children at Mulago School for the Deaf, sharing and reading Stories. It's a fantastic way to share stories as we promote literacy among Deaf children. We read for fun and enjoyment!
